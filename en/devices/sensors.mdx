Sensors are small devices placed in rooms that **detect conditions** and feed data to the SUITEe system. They are the foundation of SUITEe's automation — without sensor data, the system wouldn't know when rooms are empty, when windows are open, or what the current temperature is.

## Types of sensors

| Sensor Type | What It Detects | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Occupancy / Presence Sensor** | Whether someone is in the room | Drives energy automation — reduce heating/cooling when empty |
| **Window Sensor** | Whether a window is open or closed | Pauses heating/cooling when windows are open to prevent waste |
| **Temperature Sensor** | Current room temperature | Provides accurate temperature data for climate control |
| **Motion Sensor** | Movement in the room | Detects activity and presence for various automations |
| **Water Sensor** | Water leaks or flooding | Alerts staff immediately if water is detected (e.g., bathroom leak) |

---

## How sensors drive automations

Sensors provide the **real-time data** that makes SUITEe's automations intelligent:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Occupancy sensor → Energy savings">
    - Sensor says **"room is occupied"** → Thermostat stays in Guest Mode (comfortable)
    - Sensor says **"room is empty"** → After a delay, thermostat reduces energy consumption
    - Sensor says **"guest returned"** → Thermostat restores comfort quickly
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Window sensor → Heating/cooling control">
    - Sensor says **"window is open"** → Heating/cooling pauses immediately
    - Sensor says **"window is closed"** → Heating/cooling resumes normal operation
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Temperature sensor → Accurate climate control">
    - Provides real-time temperature readings to the thermostat
    - Ensures rooms reach the correct temperature during pre-conditioning
    - Alerts staff if a room temperature falls outside normal ranges
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Water sensor → Leak detection">
    - If water is detected on the floor → Immediate alert on the dashboard
    - Prevents water damage from bathroom leaks, burst pipes, or overflowing sinks
    - Early detection saves significant repair costs
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Sensor placement

Sensors are typically placed in these locations:

| Sensor | Typical Location |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Occupancy sensor** | Ceiling or high wall position with a clear view of the room |
| **Window sensor** | On the window frame (magnetic contact sensor) |
| **Temperature sensor** | Wall-mounted at a representative height (not near heat sources) |
| **Motion sensor** | Ceiling or corner position covering the main room area |
| **Water sensor** | Floor level near bathrooms, kitchens, or under sinks |

<Info>
  Sensors are small and discreet — most guests won't even notice them. They are designed to blend into the room environment.
</Info>

## Monitoring sensor status

From the dashboard, you can see the status of every sensor:

| Status | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Online** | Sensor is working and sending data normally |
| **Offline** | Sensor has lost connection — check battery and gateway proximity |
| **Low Battery** | Sensor battery needs replacement |
| **Triggered** | Sensor is currently detecting something (e.g., occupancy detected, window open) |

<Warning>
  **If an occupancy sensor goes offline**, the system cannot detect whether a room is occupied. This means energy automations won't work correctly for that room. Address offline sensor alerts promptly.
</Warning>

## Battery and maintenance

- Most sensors are **battery-powered** with long battery life (typically 1–2 years)
- The dashboard alerts you when a sensor's battery is low
- **No wiring** is needed — sensors are attached with adhesive or screws
- Replacement is simple: swap the old sensor for a new one and pair it in the dashboard

<Tip>
  **Setup tip:** Power on gateways and sensors approximately **30 minutes** before starting the device setup process. This gives them time to establish a stable connection.
</Tip>
